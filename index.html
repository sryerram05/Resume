What if a 5-meter-radius black hole spawned in your bedroom?

math

Short answer: you would never get to see it. The Earth would be destroyed almost instantly, and the Moon would fall in a few hours later.

Step 1: How heavy is it?

To find the black hole's mass, start with the escape-velocity relation:

M = r v22G
r = 5 m (radius of the event horizon)
G = 6.674 × 10−11 m3/(kg·s2)
v = escape velocity

First, a fun detour. Suppose you are standing 5 feet (1.524 m) from the event horizon. Using v = c·√(R / 2r) with R = 5 m gives a speed of 383,970,822 m/s, which is faster than light. Einstein's relativity says that's impossible, so the most nature allows is v = c. That also matches the definition of an event horizon: the place where escape velocity equals the speed of light.

Plugging in c = 299,792,458 m/s:

M = 5 × (299,792,458)22 × 6.674 × 10−11 ≈ 3.37 × 1027 kg

That's 3,370,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000 kg.

Step 2: The Earth

Something more than 500 times heavier than the Earth is now sitting inside it. The planet gets crushed and pulled into the black hole.

Step 3: The Moon

Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation gives the pull on the Moon:

F = G m Mr2
m = 7.35 × 1022 kg (Moon)
r ≈ 384,400 km (Earth–Moon distance)

That works out to F ≈ 1.12 × 1023 N. Using Newton's Second Law:

a = Fm ≈ 1.52 m/s2

That's the Moon's acceleration toward the black hole at t = 0, about 560 times stronger than the pull Earth used to have on it.

The Moon's orbital speed (about 1 km/s) is tiny compared with the roughly 24 km/s it would now need to stay in orbit, so it falls almost straight in. The free-fall time from astrophysics is:

t = π2 √r32GM ≈ 17,700 s ≈ 4.9 hours

So the Moon lasts about five hours. In reality, tidal forces would rip it apart before it reached the horizon.

The big picture

The Sun is still almost 600 times heavier than the black hole, so it stays the anchor of the solar system. The black hole simply takes over Earth's orbit around the Sun. Venus and Mars would feel small nudges to their orbits, and the outer planets would barely notice.

Safe to say, you will not be going to school tomorrow.
